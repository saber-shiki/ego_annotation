# Graph liveness and motion-coupling invariants

- A pose graph that reports `nfev=1`, `cost=0`, zero residuals, and zero target-frame support is a pass-through, not an optimized physical state. Treat its variables as untested inputs until factor support, active residual counts, input-output deltas, and gauge/covariance are recorded per factor family.
- Object motion coupling is admissible contact evidence only when the object trajectory is live and de-noised. On clip001850 the keyboard pose rows have only 13 direct fits in 150 frames, 65 frozen missing-pose frames, 72 ineligible frames, and jumps up to 166 mm; the measured object-motion channel is dominated by pose-fit/refit noise. Motion coupling cannot promote contact under those conditions.
- A negative motion-coupling result can be a trajectory-liveness diagnosis rather than a contact diagnosis. For clip001850, f36 has the strongest fingertip approach but only 8.6 mm keyboard response, below the direct-edge median; f46 is the largest keyboard edge but occurs during hand withdrawal and mask re-acquisition. The correct next mechanism is pose graph liveness/observability, not another contact threshold.
- `confirmed_contact` requires floor-independent provenance: motion-coupling onset from a live trajectory or GT. Signed distance, shrunken gaps, hand-depth shifts, or agent interaction labels do not populate that provenance.
